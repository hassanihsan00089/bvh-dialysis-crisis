Public health issue brief Bahawal Victoria Hospital · Bahawalpur, Pakistan Information snapshot: 13 October 2026
Dialysis capacity under pressure

25 of 36 machines are reportedly not working.

At BVH’s Kidney Centre, a recent 66News report claimed only about 11 dialysis machines remain operational while more than 100 patients may need care each day. Three newer Fresenius 5008 machines are high-quality equipment—but quantity and supplies remain the central problem.

In short: the machines are high quality, but BVH does not have enough machines or compatible disposable sets.

Why the centre matters

Regional care cannot run on a narrow margin.

The Kidney Centre was created to provide kidney-related and dialysis care for people across southern Punjab. When working capacity falls, the pressure is felt beyond Bahawalpur.

What this brief does—and does not claim

Reported figures need an official current inventory.

The 36-machine total and 25-machine failure count are presented as a media claim, not as a confirmed hospital audit. BVH or Punjab health authorities should publish a current machine-by-machine status.

The core issue: this is not a criticism of the Fresenius 5008 model. It is a call for enough machines, compatible consumables, maintenance, and transparent status reporting.
The newer machine

Why the Fresenius 5008 is advanced.

The 5008 family is designed for modern HD and HDF treatment. Features can vary by exact configuration and region; trained clinical staff decide which therapies and settings are appropriate.

Quality is not the problem.

The model offers capable therapy and monitoring. Without compatible disposables—or enough machines—even advanced equipment cannot meet patient demand.

Use only under clinical supervision

Before treatment, follow the patient’s prescription, local infection-control protocol and the exact 5008 configuration in your unit. Never bypass a safety alarm.

01

Machine setup / priming — step by step

  1. Close all clamps (saare clamps BAND karein), then open the blood-line pack.
  2. Fit the RED arterial line into the blood pump and place the line guide correctly in the pump.
  3. Fit the BLUE venous line into the venous occlusion clamp.
  4. Place the venous bubble catcher in the level detector.
  5. Insert the line into the air-bubble detector.
  6. Place the dialyzer in the holder on the RIGHT side of the machine and remove the protective caps.
  7. Connect the hydrophobic filter tightly to the venous pressure connector.
  8. Fit a 20 cc heparin syringe only if prescribed by the doctor.
  9. Open the concentrate flap on the machine’s RIGHT side, then place the RED concentrate suction tube into the SMART bag.
  10. Wait for the T1 test. A new display appears when the test is complete.
  11. Open all large RED and BLUE clamps, then start priming.
  12. Check for leaks and confirm that the solution has not changed colour.

Ye guide sirf maloomat ke liye hai — har faisla doctor ya senior technician ki nigrani mein karo.

02

Common alarms — what they mean and what to do

Arterial Pressure (ART) Alarm

Display: ART outside −80 to −250 mmHg.

Meaning: The machine is having difficulty drawing blood.

Possible causes: Needle against the vessel wall, kink/bend in line, closed clamp, or clot in the access.

Action: Correct the needle position, straighten the line, open clamps, then reset the alarm.

Venous Pressure (VEN) Alarm

Display: VEN outside +80 to +250 mmHg.

Meaning: There is resistance while returning blood to the patient.

Possible causes: Kink in the venous line, clot in the chamber, or poor needle placement.

Action: Check the line, straighten any kink, inspect for clot, and change the line if required.

TMP (Transmembrane Pressure) Alarm

Meaning: Pressure across the dialyzer membrane is too high.

Possible causes: Dialyzer clotting, excessive UF rate, or protein/fibrin build-up.

Action: Check the dialyzer. If it is clotted, replace it; reduce the UF rate.

Air Detector Alarm — highest danger

Meaning: Air or foam is present in the blood line; there is a risk of air embolism.

Action: The pump will stop. Remove air from the line, tighten all connections, then restart only when safe.

Blood Leak Alarm

Meaning: Blood cells have been detected in the dialysate; the dialyzer may have ruptured.

Action: If blood is visible in the dialysate, DO NOT return the blood. Stop treatment and fit a new dialyzer.

Conductivity Alarm

Meaning: The salt/acid balance of the dialysate is not correct.

Possible causes: Acid concentrate is empty, bicarbonate is not prepared correctly, or a line is blocked.

Action: Check the concentrate and connections. If the problem continues, remove the machine from treatment.

Temperature Alarm

Meaning: Dialysate temperature is outside the safe range; above 42°C carries a haemolysis risk.

Action: Remove the machine from treatment immediately.

UF Goal Alarm

Meaning: The UF goal is set above the limit.

Action: Reduce the goal according to the prescribed treatment plan.

Bottom line

The 5008 is the more advanced option. But a superior machine is useful only when it has compatible disposable sets, trained operators, maintenance support and enough units for the patient load.
